01 / Mission

Students learn chip design by building a chip.

Members join a technical division, contribute to one shared design, and follow it through the stages a real part goes through — architecture, RTL, verification, physical design, tapeout, and bring-up. Work is reviewed, documented, and handed forward.

We are a new organization. Our first year is about establishing the toolchain, the review process, and a project worth taping out.

A

Open to every major and every year.

B

One shared design, not scattered side projects.

C

Student-run, faculty-supported.

A parallel compute block with a small number of clusters, a shared cache hierarchy, and a host interface. Sized so a student team can verify it and close timing on it.

About

A student organization for people who want to design chips.

Rev Silicon was founded to give Texas A&M students a path into semiconductor engineering that ends at silicon, not at a simulation waveform.

01 / What we are

We are a technical club organized like a small design team. Members join a division, take assignments on the club's project, and review each other's work. Officers run the schedule and the toolchain; faculty advise.

Meetings mix short teaching sessions with working time. New members start with a guided task inside a division and move to owning a block once they are comfortable.

02 / Why it matters

Coursework stops early. The industry does not.

01

The flow is the skill. Writing RTL is a fraction of the job. Verification, timing closure, and signoff are where designs succeed or fail, and they are rarely taught end to end.

02

Open tools changed access. Open architectures, open EDA, and academic shuttles put a real tapeout within reach of an organized student team.

03

Teams beat individuals. A chip is a group deliverable. Learning to hand off clean work is as valuable as any single technical skill.

03 / Divisions

Six areas of work.

D-01

Architecture

Microarchitecture studies, performance modeling, and the specification the rest of the flow builds from.

D-02

RTL / Digital design

Implementation in a hardware description language, lint clean, synthesis clean, reviewed.

D-03

Verification

Testbenches, directed and random stimulus, coverage goals, and regression discipline.

D-04

Physical design

Floorplanning, place and route, timing and power closure, and signoff checks.

D-05

Tooling / Infrastructure

Build scripts, continuous integration, environment setup, and keeping the flow reproducible.

D-06

Project / Research

Scope, schedule, documentation, and the reading that keeps the design grounded.

Join

Start where you are.

Membership is open. You do not need chip design experience — you need to be willing to learn a flow that takes time.

01 / Who should join

Curious beginners

You have not written RTL yet. You will start with a guided task and a mentor in one division.

Coursework-ready

You have taken digital design or computer architecture and want to apply it to something that ships.

Adjacent skills

Software, scripting, PCB, or documentation. Every one of those is load-bearing on a tapeout.
