Why this project

Most students never see a design past simulation. Carrying one block through physical design and tapeout teaches the parts of the job coursework leaves out — and leaves the next cohort something to extend.

ABOUT

A student organization for people who want to design chips.

Founded at Texas A&M to give students a path into semiconductor engineering that ends at silicon.

01

The flow is the skill. Verification, timing closure, and signoff decide whether a design works.

02

Open architectures and academic shuttles put a real tapeout within reach of a student team.

03

A chip is a group deliverable. Clean handoffs matter as much as any single skill.

DIVISIONS

Six areas of work.

D-01

Architecture

Microarchitecture studies and the specification the flow builds from.

D-02

RTL / Digital design

Implementation in HDL — lint clean, synthesis clean, reviewed.

D-03

Verification

Testbenches, stimulus, coverage goals, regression discipline.

D-04

Physical design

Floorplanning, place and route, timing and power closure.

D-05

Tooling

Build scripts, CI, and keeping the flow reproducible.

D-06

Project / Research

Scope, schedule, documentation, and the reading behind it.

STRUCTURE

Student-run, faculty-advised.

An officer board sets the schedule and owns the toolchain. Division leads run weekly work sessions. Members take assignments on the shared design.
